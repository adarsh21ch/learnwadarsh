# Sourcing standard

The risk this file exists to prevent: another creator picks up my reel, finds one wrong
number, and makes a debunk video. Their video outperforms mine. That clip follows me
forever. **One bad stat costs more than ten good reels earn.**

So: a weaker claim that is true always beats a stronger claim that is shaky.

---

## Source tiers

### Tier 1 — Primary. Say it on camera without flinching.
- Government: PIB, MoSPI / NSO, RBI, Census, ministry reports, Economic Survey
- Regulators: SEBI, TRAI, IRDAI, CCI, FSSAI
- Company filings: BSE/NSE disclosures, annual reports, investor decks
- Courts: actual judgments, not reporting about judgments
- Peer-reviewed journals, with the DOI
- Multilateral raw data: WHO, World Bank, UN, IMF

### Tier 2 — Reliable secondary. Usable, but trace it back first.
- Established newsrooms **reporting a named primary source** — then go read that source
- Industry research: Redseer, Kantar, Nielsen, Bain, RedSeer, IAMAI — cite the study name
- Named expert quoted on record in a reputable outlet

> Rule for Tier 2: if the article says "according to X", my job is to go find X.
> I cite X, not the article.

### Tier 3 — NEVER stated as fact on camera.
- **The agency's own brief.** A brief is a *claim*, not a source. It is the thing being checked.
- Other creators' videos and reels
- Wikipedia — useful to *find* primary sources, never to cite
- Blog aggregators, "as per reports", unsourced infographics
- Forwarded WhatsApp / X threads
- **AI output, including Claude's.** If I can't click through to a real source, it doesn't ship.

---

## The rules

1. **Every number on camera has a Tier 1 or Tier 2 link in `FACTCHECK.md`.** No link, no claim.
2. **Date every figure.** "2024 mein" — not "currently", not "aaj kal". Old data stated as
   present-tense is the most common way a true number becomes a false claim.
3. **Never round in my own favour.** 61% is "61%", not "almost 70%".
4. **Name the source out loud when the number is spicy.** *"RBI ke apne data ke hisaab se…"*
   This is the single best debunk shield there is — it moves the burden onto the source,
   and a debunker now has to argue with RBI, not with me.
5. **Attribute contested claims, don't assert them.** *"Company ka claim hai ki…"* not *"Ye hai…"*
6. **Keep receipts.** Archive the source into `topics/<slug>/sources/` — PDF or screenshot,
   with the access date. Links rot; a debunk arrives six months later.
7. **State the counter-argument somewhere in the reel.** One line is enough. It pre-empts the
   dunk *and* it makes the reel more credible, not less.
8. **If it can't be verified, it gets cut.** Not softened. Cut.

---

## Pre-record check

Ask before the camera turns on:

- [ ] Could a hostile creator freeze **any single frame** and dunk on it?
- [ ] Does every number have a **date**, a **denominator**, and a **named source**?
- [ ] Am I saying "study says" without naming the study? → name it or cut it
- [ ] Am I stating a Tier-3 claim in Tier-1 tone?
- [ ] Do I know the **strongest argument against me** — and is it in the reel?
- [ ] Are the receipts archived, so I can answer a challenge **within the hour**?

Speed matters on that last one. An unanswered debunk becomes the truth by default.

---

## If I get challenged anyway

1. Check first — genuinely. If they're right, correct it fast and publicly. A quick
   correction costs far less than a defended mistake.
2. If they're wrong: reply once, with the primary source link. Don't fight in the comments.
3. Either way, log it in `published/log.md` so the same claim never ships twice.
