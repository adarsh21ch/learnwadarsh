# Research — India's NGO ecosystem, FCRA, and the "startup vs NGO" argument

> Written for me, not the audience. Test: can I talk about this unscripted for 10 minutes?

## 1. The topic from zero

Two separate registries are being compared:

**NGO Darpan** — a NITI Aayog portal launched 2015. Voluntary. An NGO signs up mainly to get a
Unique ID, which it needs to receive government grants and, after Form CSR-1 was introduced, to
receive CSR money. So registrations surged for **funding-access reasons**, not because activism grew.
It is a cumulative list. Nothing removes a dormant NGO.

**DPIIT Startup Recognition** — a Startup India certification, also voluntary. Eligibility: under
10 years old, turnover under ₹100 cr, incorporated as Pvt Ltd/LLP/registered partnership, working
on innovation or a scalable model. ~1.98 lakh recognised as of 31 Oct 2025. Recognition **lapses**
at 10 years.

So: one list only accumulates, the other constantly sheds. Dividing them produces a number that
rises every year by construction.

**The number nobody in the brief mentions:** India has **7.83 crore enterprises on Udyam**. That's
the count of Indian businesses. Against it, 6 lakh NGOs is ~1 per 130 firms.

## 2. Why it's a story now
FCRA enforcement has been heavy and continuous — 20,693 FCRA licences cancelled over the past
decade; ~16,301 valid as of July 2023. Sonam Wangchuk's NGO lost its FCRA certificate in Sept 2025.
Each cancellation revives the argument.

## 3. The genuinely strong parts of the case

These are real and I can defend them:

- **Accountability is genuinely poor.** CBI's 2015 Supreme Court affidavit mapped ~31 lakh societies
  and found only a small fraction filed balance sheets. That's a real governance failure.
- **Manmohan Singh, Feb 2012**, in a *Science* interview, blamed US-based NGOs for stalling
  Kudankulam. This matters because it kills the "this is one party's talking point" response —
  a Congress PM said it first. **Strongest fact in the deck.**
- **Environics Trust**: the IT Department told the Supreme Court it had bank records of payments to
  protest participants and agreements with foreign entities. FCRA and 12A registrations cancelled.
  Still sub judice — SC barred a final order pending directions. So: allegation, credible, not proven.
- **The 2014 IB report exists** and did name coal, nuclear, GM crops, POSCO, Vedanta, Narmada.

## 4. What the brief leaves out

**a) The ratio is an artifact of the denominator.** Covered in FACTCHECK. This is the whole ballgame.

**b) Why the Northeast actually has many NGOs.** Structural, not sinister:
   - Low state capacity and difficult terrain — government delivery is weak, so schemes are
     *deliberately routed through* NGOs
   - Dense church and community-organisation networks that register as societies. In the NE these
     are the mainstream social infrastructure, not fringe groups
   - Sixth Schedule autonomous councils and traditional bodies that register as societies
   - Manipur has ~740 NGOs per million — the highest per-capita density in India
   - And these are India's least industrialised states, which is exactly why they have few startups

   Same fact, two readings. The brief picks one and never mentions the other exists.

**c) The 2–3% GDP figure has no published methodology.** EPW ran
*"The Intelligence Bureau and Its Not Very Intelligent Report on NGOs"* (2014). Core objection:
attributing GDP-growth deltas to protest activity isn't something the best econometrics can do,
let alone an intelligence report with no stated method.

**d) The timeline contradicts the thesis.** NGO registrations exploded across the same decade India
grew 6–8% and became the world's third-largest startup ecosystem. If NGOs were throttling growth
at 2–3%, that should show up somewhere. It doesn't.

**e) NGOs also build.** Immunisation, midday meals, disaster response, microfinance, SHGs, literacy.
The "gatekeepers vs builders" frame assumes the entire sector is advocacy. Advocacy is a slice.

## 5. Questions a sharp viewer will ask

1. **"Isn't 2.5:1 just because DPIIT recognition is a scheme, not a business count?"**
   → Yes. That's the strongest objection and it's correct. Real ratio vs Udyam ≈ 1:130.
2. **"Sikkim has how many startups?"** → 11. The ratio says more about startups than NGOs.
3. **"Where's the 37 lakh from?"** → I can't source it. CBI's figure is 31 lakh. Ask the agency.
4. **"Did the IB show its working on 2–3%?"** → No. That's the documented criticism.
5. **"Are you saying NGOs shouldn't exist?"** → No. The defensible claim is about **transparency**:
   most registered NGOs file no accounts. That's it.
6. **"Isn't this just targeting Christians in the Northeast?"** → Which is why claim 13 is out.

## 6. Where I actually land

There is a real, defensible reel here — but it is **not** the one the brief describes.

- Defensible: *most of India's registered NGOs file no public accounts, and nobody can tell you how
  many are even alive.* True, sourced to CBI, genuinely interesting, and nobody can debunk it.
- Not defensible: *NGO density on the border is a security threat.* Built on a broken ratio, and the
  numbers collapse the moment someone opens the DPIIT state table.
