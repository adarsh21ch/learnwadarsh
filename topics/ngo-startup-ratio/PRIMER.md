# Primer — India's NGO sector, FCRA, and the growth argument

The full briefing. Test: can I hold a 10-minute unscripted conversation on this?

---

## 1. "NGO" is not one thing — it's three layers stacked

Most people, including most commentators, mix these up. Getting them straight is what makes
me sound like I know the subject.

**Layer 1 — How it's born (registration)**
| Route | Law | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| Society | Societies Registration Act, **1860** | Most common. Membership bodies, clubs, church councils |
| Trust | Indian Trusts Act, **1882** | Family/endowment charities |
| Section 8 Company | Companies Act, **2013** | Professionalised NGOs. Strictest reporting |

The "31 lakh" CBI figure counts **societies** — the loosest of the three, registered by state
registrars since 1860, with nothing that ever removes a dead one.

**Layer 2 — Tax status (Income Tax Act)**
- **12A/12AB** — the organisation's own income is exempt
- **80G** — the *donor* gets a deduction
These are what the IT Department cancels when it acts. Losing 12A is often fatal.

**Layer 3 — Foreign money (FCRA)**
A separate licence, from the **Home Ministry**, not the Tax Department. Most NGOs never have it.
~16,301 valid FCRA licences as of July 2023 — against lakhs of registered NGOs.

> **Key insight:** the brief slides between layers. "31 lakh NGOs" is Layer 1. "Foreign-funded
> NGOs stalling projects" is Layer 3 — a group ~200× smaller. They are not the same population.

## 2. The money map

| Source | Scale | Governed by |
|---|---|---|
| **CSR** | **₹34,909 crore** in FY24, 27,188 companies | Companies Act s.135 — 2% of avg net profit |
| Government grants | Large, scheme-routed | Ministry budgets |
| Domestic donations | Largest but unmeasured | 12A/80G |
| **Foreign contribution** | The contested slice | **FCRA** |

**Why NGO Darpan registrations exploded:** Form CSR-1 made a Darpan Unique ID effectively
necessary to receive CSR money and government grants. So the curve tracks **funding access
rules**, not a surge in activism. This single fact undercuts the "institutional tilt" story.

## 3. FCRA — the mechanism the brief is arguing for

**1976** — enacted during the Emergency, over concerns about foreign money in Indian politics.
**2010** — replaced by the current Act.
**2020 Amendment** — the big one. Four changes that reshaped the sector:
1. **Sub-granting banned** — an FCRA holder can no longer pass foreign funds to another
   organisation, even another FCRA holder. This killed the model where one large NGO funded
   hundreds of small grassroots ones.
2. **Admin expenses capped at 20%** (was 50%) — salaries, rent, travel, legal.
3. **One bank branch** — all foreign contribution must land in an FCRA account at the
   **State Bank of India, New Delhi Main Branch**.
4. **Aadhaar mandatory** for all office bearers.

**Supreme Court upheld these in *Noel Harper v. Union of India* (April 2022)**, holding that
there is no fundamental right to receive foreign contribution. Strong point for the pro-
regulation side, and it's a court ruling, not an opinion.

## 4. The crackdown, in numbers and names

- **20,693** FCRA licences cancelled over the past decade
- **16,301** valid as of July 2023
- Named: Greenpeace India (2015) · Compassion International (2017) · Amnesty India
  (halted operations 2020) · Missionaries of Charity (denied Dec 2021, restored Jan 2022) ·
  Oxfam India (2022) · Centre for Policy Research (2024) · Sonam Wangchuk's org (Sept 2025)

That list is the argument in miniature: some are alleged fund-diverters, some are research
institutions, one is Mother Teresa's order. Which is exactly why both sides can point at it.

## 5. The startup side

**DPIIT recognition** is a Startup India certificate, not a business count:
- under 10 years old · turnover under ₹100 cr · Pvt Ltd / LLP / registered partnership
- must **apply**; recognition **lapses** at 10 years
- ~1,97,692 recognised (31 Oct 2025)

**Actual Indian businesses: 7.83 crore on Udyam.**

NE states have few DPIIT startups for ordinary reasons — small populations, thin formal
economies, little VC presence, low awareness of the scheme. Sikkim: **11**.

## 6. The honest case on each side

**For tighter regulation**
- Only a small fraction of registered NGOs file accounts (CBI to Supreme Court)
- Real diversion cases exist and are documented — Environics Trust is in court now
- Sovereignty: foreign money shaping domestic policy is a legitimate democratic concern,
  and every major country regulates it
- The Supreme Court has upheld the framework

**Against overreach**
- The 2020 sub-granting ban hit small grassroots NGOs hardest — they had no independent
  FCRA and depended on re-granting. Casualties were service deliverers, not activists
- Enforcement looks selective: research bodies and critics feature heavily
- Blanket measures don't distinguish a shell from a hospital
- Courts have granted relief in several cases, so "cancelled" ≠ "guilty"

**Both are true at once.** That's what makes it a real debate rather than a slogan.

## 7. Why the Northeast genuinely has many NGOs

- Weak state capacity and hard terrain → government **deliberately routes schemes through NGOs**
- Dense church and community networks registered as societies — mainstream social
  infrastructure there, not fringe
- Sixth Schedule autonomous councils and traditional bodies register as societies
- Manipur: ~740 NGOs per million, highest per-capita density in India
- These are also India's least industrialised states — which is *why* startup counts are tiny

## 8. What IS true about project delays

The aggregate "2–3% of GDP" has no published method. But specific delays are real and citable:
- **POSCO** abandoned its ₹52,000 crore Odisha steel project (2017) after a decade of resistance
- **Vedanta / Niyamgiri** — Supreme Court ordered gram sabha consent (2013); the councils voted no
- **Kudankulam** — commissioning delayed by years of protest

So: *"projects get delayed and it costs money"* is defensible. *"NGOs cost India 2–3% of GDP"*
is not. Know the difference — it's where a debunker will test me.

## 9. Vocabulary
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Darpan ID** | NITI Aayog unique ID; needed for CSR and government grants |
| **12A / 80G** | Org's tax exemption / donor's deduction |
| **FCRA** | Home Ministry licence to receive foreign funds |
| **Sub-granting** | Passing foreign funds on — banned since 2020 |
| **UBO** | Ultimate Beneficial Owner — who really controls the money |
| **Section 8** | Not-for-profit company under Companies Act 2013 |
| **Sixth Schedule** | Autonomous tribal council areas in the NE |

## 10. Landmines
1. Don't conflate "31 lakh NGOs" with "foreign-funded NGOs" — ~200× different populations
2. Don't say the IB number as fact — attribute, always
3. Don't name Greenpeace as guilty — republication is publication
4. Never touch the conversion claim
5. Don't imply NE NGOs are inherently suspect — church bodies there run schools and clinics
6. Don't say "NGOs" when I mean "some NGOs"
