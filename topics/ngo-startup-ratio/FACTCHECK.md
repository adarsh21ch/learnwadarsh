## 🔴 The Fatal Flaw — this is what gets you debunked

**The denominator is a certification scheme, not a count of businesses.**

"DPIIT-recognised startup" is a voluntary scheme with eligibility rules: under 10 years old,
turnover under ₹100 cr, incorporated as Pvt Ltd / LLP / registered partnership, working on
innovation. ~2 lakh companies hold it. And recognition **expires** — cross 10 years and you
drop off the count.

India actually has **7.83 crore enterprises registered on Udyam** (PIB, Feb 2026).

> 6 lakh NGOs ÷ 7.83 crore enterprises ≈ **1 NGO per 130 businesses.**

The 2.5:1 exists only because the brief picked the narrowest possible definition of "builder"
and the broadest possible definition of "NGO." One registry only grows; the other churns.

**The state map has the same problem, worse.** Sikkim's 14.1 is roughly 155 NGOs ÷ **11 startups**.
Not 155 vs 1,100. Eleven. In a state of ~7 lakh people. The map is measuring **startup scarcity**
and labelling it NGO excess. Ladakh and Arunachal are the same story.

Honest caveat in the other direction: Punjab (5.0) and West Bengal (4.9) are large states, so
"small population" doesn't explain those two. Don't overclaim this counter-argument either.

## Debunk exposure

**Strongest attack a rival creator can make, in one line:**
> *"India has 7.83 crore registered businesses. He compared NGOs to a certification scheme
> only 2 lakh companies applied for — and called Sikkim's ELEVEN startups a national security threat."*

**Second attack:**
> *"The 2–3% GDP number is from a 2014 IB report that never published a methodology, and EPW
> demolished it the same month."*

**How the script must handle it:** attribute every contested claim to its source out loud,
drop claims 3/9/10/12/13 entirely, and state the denominator caveat inside the reel.
